Clinical Training & OPD

From clinic visits in the first year to a 12-month rotatory internship: how RCP students learn to care for patients.

Physiotherapy students learn their most important lessons with patients. Under the NCAHP competency-based curriculum, clinical training begins in the first year and grows step by step: students first observe, then assist and finally assess and treat under supervision, until by the end of the internship they can assess, diagnose and treat patients independently.

The clinical learning ladder

Each year has its own clinical course with set hours in the scheme of study, and each one asks a little more of the student.

  1. Year 1 · Clinic Orientation & Visit (BPT-110) · 150 hours

    Part of the foundation course. Students visit the whole chain of healthcare delivery: sub-centre, PHC, CHC, sub-district and district hospitals, a medical college, and private hospitals and clinics. They also meet the village panchayat and front-line health workers.

  2. Year 2 · Clinical Observation (BPT-209) · 210 hours

    Rotating postings in physiotherapy OPDs and hospital wards, where students observe patient care and keep a case portfolio or diary.

  3. Year 3 · Clinical Education (BPT-308) · 300 hours

    Supervised training in wards and OPDs: bedside approach, assessment, special tests, indications and contraindications, treatment dosage and outcome measures. Case presentations, case discussions and critique enquiry become a regular part of learning.

  4. Year 4 · Clinical Rotation (BPT-409) · 390 hours

    A supervised rotation through ten clinical areas. Students decide treatment parameters and dosage, use outcome measures and apply evidence-based practice for real patients.

  5. Year 5 · Rotatory Clinical Internship · 12 months

    Full-time postings averaging 7 hours a day, 6 days a week, for a minimum of 2,016 hours, including a 2-month rural posting. Interns keep a log book, present at least twelve cases and complete a supervised project. About the internship.

Skills before patients. Every practical skill is first taught in the laboratories with the DOAP model (Demonstrate, Observe, Assist, Perform), and special tests are practised on a healthy model before they are used on patients. Competence is assessed at rising levels, from knows and knows how to shows how and finally performs independently.

The physiotherapy OPD

NCAHP norms require every BPT college to have its own well-equipped physiotherapy out-patient department on the college premises; an OPD at a tie-up hospital does not count. RCP's on-campus OPD serves musculoskeletal, neurological, cardio-respiratory, sports, women's health and community physiotherapy patients, and gives students supervised clinical exposure from early in the course.

  • Reception and waiting area
  • Consultation rooms
  • Electrotherapy unit with separate chambers
  • Cryotherapy, wax bath and hydrocollator area
  • Treatment cabins
  • Exercise-therapy gymnasium
  • Parallel bars, gait training and mat exercise space
  • Hydrotherapy unit

The norms also call for a student-to-patient ratio of 1:5 in the hospital, and for a community or extension centre in a nearby rural or semi-urban area in addition to the campus OPD. OPD facilities

Hospital postings

Ward, ICU and specialty postings take place in hospital settings arranged in line with the NCAHP minimum standards for clinical training. The norms set out what a BPT college must provide:

  • A college on the premises of an NMC-recognised medical college needs no other hospital attachment. Otherwise it must show an own or attached hospital for clinical training.
  • A college may tie up with at most five hospitals with in-patient and out-patient facilities. Each tie-up hospital may serve no more than two colleges, and its MoU must state the specialties, patient loads and equipment available for training.
  • The hospital must be within 20 km of the college, and a bus service for students is mandatory if it is more than 1 km away.
  • The college must provide physiotherapy faculty to supervise and teach students in the hospital.

NCAHP norm: a 250-bed own or attached hospital for the clinical training of 50 students, at a student-to-bed ratio of 1:5, within a 20 km radius. This is the national minimum standard as stated in the Curriculum Handbook of Physiotherapy (2025), not a description of RCP's hospital arrangements. RGUHS and State Government regulations prevail where they differ.

Specialties required in clinical training

  • Surgery, including plastic surgery and burns

  • Neurology / neurosurgery

Community outreach

Physiotherapy is needed as much in villages as in hospitals. Community work runs through the course, from the first-year clinic visits to the rural internship posting, and is led by the Department of Community Physiotherapy & Rehabilitation.

PHC and rehabilitation centre visits

Field visits to urban and rural primary health centres and regional rehabilitation training centres.

Camps and disability surveys

Regular mobile camps, with disability surveys and screening in villages.

Low-cost assistive devices

Prescribing ambulatory and assistive devices, and making low-cost devices from locally available materials.

Health education

Community education programmes on health and disability, using the portable equipment of the community laboratory.

During the internship, a 2-month rural posting, preferably at a government CHC, PHC or Rural Rehabilitation Centre, is coordinated by the State Council.

Patient-care values

Good clinical skills are only half of patient care. Throughout their postings, students are expected to:

  • Explain each assessment and treatment and obtain consent
  • Respect patients' privacy, confidentiality and dignity
  • Communicate with empathy and listen carefully
  • Document every assessment and session in SOAP format
  • Follow infection control and safety procedures
  • Use outcome measures and change treatment when needed
  • Recognise when physiotherapy is not indicated and refer on
  • Work respectfully with doctors, nurses and the wider team

These values are taught formally in Physiotherapy Ethics, Medico-Legal Aspects, Management & Administration (BPT-406), which covers informed consent, confidentiality, negligence and patients' rights.
