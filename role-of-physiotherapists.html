Role of Physiotherapists

A first-contact, science-based profession that helps people of every age move, function and live better.

Physiotherapy covers the whole range of care, from health promotion and prevention through to rehabilitation, for individuals and for whole populations at every stage of life. This page explains the role of the physiotherapist as described in the NCAHP Curriculum Handbook of Physiotherapy (2025), the national curriculum that RCP follows.

About physiotherapy

Physiotherapists diagnose movement dysfunction through careful examination and evaluation, whatever its cause. They then give skilled therapeutic treatment to improve physical function and help each person reach the best possible quality of life.

They combine detailed knowledge of the human body and how it works with specialised hands-on clinical skills. They use these to assess, diagnose and treat physical problems caused by disorders, illness, injury, trauma or disability. Physiotherapists work independently and as members of the multidisciplinary rehabilitation and habilitation team. After the necessary assessment, evaluation and investigations, they prescribe and carry out treatment programmes that help people gain, keep or regain the best possible function.

A first point of contact. Physiotherapists are an essential part of primary care: people with impairments or functional limitations can come to a physiotherapist directly as their way into the healthcare system. Physiotherapists also refer patients to other professionals and receive referrals from them, and work as case managers, teachers, researchers and consultants.

What physiotherapists do

Physiotherapists follow their own code of ethical principles. Their work may include any of the following:

Promote health

Promoting the health and well-being of individuals and the public, with emphasis on physical activity and exercise.

Prevent

Preventing impairments, activity limitations, participation restrictions and disabilities in people whose movement is at risk from health, socio-economic, environmental or lifestyle factors.

Assess and diagnose

Evaluating the person, prescribing the investigations needed to diagnose physical dysfunction, and prescribing physiotherapy treatment plans.

Treat

Restoring the body systems essential to movement, maximising function and recovery, minimising incapacity, and improving quality of life, independent living and the ability to work.

Remove barriers

Adapting environmental, home and workplace access so that people can take full part in their normal roles in society.

Shape policy

Contributing to local, national and international health policies and public health strategies.

Scope and need for physiotherapists in India

Healthcare is moving quickly towards a patient-centred model delivered by multidisciplinary teams. India faces more chronic disease, an ageing population and more lifestyle-related illness, so demand for rehabilitative and therapeutic services is growing fast. Physiotherapists improve patients' quality of life by promoting mobility, managing pain and building functional independence, which also reduces the load on medical and surgical treatment.

As the Government of India works towards Universal Health Coverage and expands hospitals, clinics, wellness centres and academic institutions, physiotherapists are essential members of multidisciplinary teams at every level of healthcare delivery. Growing awareness of preventive healthcare is increasing demand in both rural and urban areas.

For example, the National guidelines for prevention and management of stroke note that physiotherapists greatly improve the management of stroke survivors and help them return to normal life sooner.

The breadth of physiotherapy practice

  • Across the whole span of human development, from newborn to old age
  • With patients who have complex and challenging physical problems caused by systemic illness, disease, disorders or trauma
  • Towards health promotion and disease prevention
  • In the assessment, diagnosis, management and evaluation of treatments and treatment protocols
  • In the patient's home, the community, healthcare facilities (including those in educational institutions and corporate or industrial settings), primary care centres and tertiary care hospitals
  • With an understanding of the health issues linked to India's different economic conditions and cultural norms

Who benefits from physiotherapy?

The handbook gives these examples, and says the list of people who can benefit is endless:

  • People of all ages with mobility difficulties
  • Sportspersons
  • Pregnant women
  • People with soft-tissue injuries
  • Post-surgical patients
  • Patients with cardiac and pulmonary conditions
  • The elderly
  • Cancer patients
  • People with chronic conditions such as diabetes
  • People with neuropathic pain
  • Amputees
  • People with other lifestyle disorders

Title and recognition

Dr … PTPrescribed title: prefix "Dr" and suffix "PT"
ISCO 2264International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-08), ILO
NCAHP Act, 2021Brings 56 allied and healthcare professions under regulation
BPTThe single entry pathway into the profession
  • The professional responsible for physiotherapy treatment and management in the healthcare team is recognised as a physiotherapist (sometimes called a physical therapist). "Physiotherapist" is the internationally used name and also applies in India.
  • The Commission recognises as a physiotherapist any healthcare professional who has earned a Bachelor of Physiotherapy from a recognised university or college under the Commission's regulations.
  • The handbook states: "The recommended title thus stands as the 'Physiotherapist' with the Prefix 'Dr' and suffix 'PT'." A qualified physiotherapist therefore writes their name as, for example, Dr [Name], PT.
  • Under ISCO-08, physiotherapists are classified as healthcare professionals because of their duties, their responsibility for patient care and the length of their entry-level training.
  • Under the NCAHP Act, 2021, a healthcare professional must hold a qualification of at least 3,600 hours, spread over three to six years.
  • The Government aims to phase out Diploma and PG Diploma courses in physiotherapy and promote only bachelor's and master's degrees. To work as a Lecturer or Assistant Professor, a candidate must hold a master's degree.

Physiotherapist (NCAHP definition): a professional who practises physiotherapy. A physiotherapist carries out a comprehensive examination and appropriate investigations, and provides treatment and advice to anyone with movement or functional dysfunction, malfunction, disorder, disability, healing needs or pain from trauma and disease, using physical modalities, including exercise, mobilisation, manipulation, electrical and thermal agents and other electrotherapeutics, for prevention, screening, diagnosis, treatment, health promotion and fitness.

Scope of the job and areas of intervention

Physiotherapy has expanded into specialties such as early intervention, preventive care, sports physiotherapy, ergonomics, community-based rehabilitation and manual therapy. The handbook expects posts and special units in these areas at every level, from Primary Health Centres to Medical College Hospitals, in the public and private sectors, with physiotherapy networked with doctors, dentists, nurses and other allied health professionals.

Prevention and primary care

  • Early identification and intervention centres: early exercise programmes for orthopaedic, neurological and paediatric conditions; screening of school children and rural populations at PHCs
  • Rural preventive care: preventive cardiac care (structured METS exercise), disability screening camps and diabetic care programmes
  • Ergonomics care: assessing workers and computer users to prevent posture- and work-related problems
  • IT industry: posture and body-mechanics screening, fitness and injury prevention for sedentary work
  • Schools and colleges: sports training needs, injury prevention, safe return to play and inclusive physical education

Specialised clinical care

  • Women's health: antenatal and postnatal care, pelvic floor health, menstrual health, osteoporosis prevention and chronic pelvic pain
  • Paediatric care units: premature and critically ill newborns, developmental delay, neuromuscular disorders and family education
  • Geriatric health: arthritis, balance disorders, dementia, fall prevention, and gait and balance training
  • Oncology and cancer survivors: pre-operative exercise, cancer-related fatigue, pain and lymphoedema, and rehabilitation
  • Emergency care and ICUs: respiratory care, early mobilisation and mechanical ventilation management
  • Sports injury care: immediate and rehabilitative care for sportspersons, including in rural areas

Community and national service

  • Community-based rehabilitation (CBR): home programmes and home modifications suited to each community
  • Disaster management teams: triage, first aid, acute care, fracture and amputation management and rehabilitation, with NGOs and government agencies
  • Indian Defence Services: injury prevention, rehabilitation, fitness and conditioning in military hospitals and field hospitals

Beyond the clinic

  • Research scientists: studying the effectiveness and mechanisms of health interventions
  • Hospital administration: budgets, staff, resources and programme evaluation
  • Space and oceanography: keeping astronauts and deep-sea divers healthy
  • Indian Administrative Services: health policy, planning and management, for example under the National Health Mission and Ayushman Bharat
  • Insurance: policy review, claims management and data analysis

Extended-scope practice

Extended Scope Physiotherapists are advanced physiotherapists with many years of clinical experience who work beyond the recognised scope of physiotherapy practice. Their work includes:

  • Musculoskeletal ultrasound scanning
  • Injection therapy
  • Pain Management Programmes
  • Intramuscular dry needling
  • Manual therapy (Mulligan, Maitland, McKenzie, Cyriax)
  • Taping techniques
  • Aquatic therapy
  • Fascia therapy
  • Ball and band therapy
  • Chiropractic and osteopathy

Career opportunities for BPT graduates

The handbook lists employers including hospitals, nursing homes, sports teams, fitness centres, community rehabilitation, health planning boards and health promotion services, in both the private and public sectors, as well as independent physiotherapy clinics. The BPT coursework qualifies graduates to carry out independent physiotherapy assessment and treatment in any healthcare centre in India.

Graduates are encouraged to go on to further qualifications and continuing professional education. Clinical, academic and research career tracks, and the route through MPT and PhD, are described on the Courses Offered page.
